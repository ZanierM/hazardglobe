Convection currents

  1. Heat from the core warms rock at the bottom of the mantle.
  2. The hot rock becomes less dense and rises slowly.
  3. Near the crust it spreads out sideways, cools and sinks again.
  4. This circular movement drags the plates above it. Where currents rise, plates move apart. Where they sink, plates move together.
cooler → hotter rock
Much of the core's heat comes from the decay of radioactive elements. Scientists now think plates are also pushed and pulled by their own weight: ridge push at mid-ocean ridges and slab pull where a plate sinks. The stripes on the ocean floor record changes in Earth's magnetic field as new rock forms at the ridge.
